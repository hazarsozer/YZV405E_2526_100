01 — The Task Rank by Meaning, Not by Look
Rank five images by how well they capture what the idiom actually means.

An idiomatic expression — bad apple, cold feet, broken heart — can be read literally or figuratively. Only the surrounding sentence tells which.

The visual choice depends on context, not the phrase.

02 — Architecture A Four-Phase Frozen Pipeline
Translate, paraphrase, classify, then rank.
Every encoder is frozen. The only trained component is a calibrated logistic regression — 25 KB on disk.

03 — The Pivot What Actually Moved the Score
The captions beat the vision encoder. By a lot.

Our original SigLIP2 image–text pipeline scored 0.30. Replacing it with BGE-M3 text matching against the English captions jumped to 0.37 — and nothing we tried later could improve on it.

  • SigLIP2's text encoder is weak on non-English input and on idiom-laden language, even after NLLB translation.
  • Image captions, in contrast, are always in English — a clean, single-language channel.
  • BGE-M3 is built for multilingual semantic similarity. The query (translated + paraphrased) lives in the same space as the captions.
  • Adding SigLIP2 image features back on top of BGE-M3 captions did not help — the text channel already dominates.

05 — Ablations Six Rounds, One Ceiling
We tried six different ways to break 0.37. None worked.
Each round targeted a different component — paraphraser, similarity model, threshold, signal mixture. The ceiling held.
Round 1 · Backbone

BGE captions beat SigLIP2 image–text

SigLIP2's text encoder is weak on non-EN and idiom-heavy input. Switching to BGE-M3 matching against English captions jumped from 0.30 → 0.37.

SigLIP2 → BGE · 0.30 → 0.37
Round 2 · Paraphraser

Bigger paraphraser doesn't help

Phi-3.5-mini (3.8 B, 4-bit) vs. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (4-bit) — identical 0.37. Paraphrase quality is not the bottleneck.

Phi-3.5 ≡ Qwen-7B · 0.37 = 0.37
Round 3 · Similarity

Cross-encoder hurts

BGE-reranker-v2-m3 cross-encoder, which usually beats bi-encoders on retrieval, dropped to 0.35. Diagnostic: the task is not standard retrieval.

cross-enc · 0.35 (−0.02)
Round 4 · Data pipeline

PT-BR fix & soft blend

Routing PT-BR through NLLB (was accidentally skipped) and softly blending literal/idiomatic scores at the boundary — both stayed at 0.36–0.37.

no-op · 0.36–0.37
Round 5 · Threshold τ

Threshold sweep is flat

Scanning τ ∈ {0.50, 0.55, 0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85} — flat 0.37 for any τ ≥ 0.60. Below 0.60 it slightly hurts.

τ ≥ 0.60 · 0.37 (flat)
Round 6 · Caption enrichment

Idiom-aware captions hurt

Augmenting each caption with a Phi-3.5 sentence describing the symbolic concept dropped pure enrichment to 0.33. The signal gets contaminated.

enriched · 0.33 (−0.04)
Conclusion 0.37 is a robust ceiling for text-caption matching on this task — the limit is not the model, it is the modality.

06 — Conclusions The Visual Metaphor Gap
Captions describe scenes. Idioms describe concepts. That is the gap.
A figurative image of "bad apple" shows one rotten apple corrupting others. Its caption describes the apples — not the concept of bad influence. No text model bridges that.

What worked

  • Classifier-gated query selection — using paraphrase for idiomatic sentences, original for literal. +0.07 over the proposed pipeline.
  • BGE-M3 over SigLIP2 for the text channel — multilingual + English-caption alignment beats fragmented multilingual vision-text.
  • 94.1% sense accuracy from a single calibrated LR head on top of frozen BGE-M3.
  • Lean compute — 3.5 GB peak VRAM, frozen encoders, full 15-language inference in minutes.

Limits & what's next

  • 0.37 ≪ 0.83 human — six ablation rounds confirmed the ceiling is structural, not engineering.
  • The next jump needs cross-modal reasoning over images, not better text-text similarity.
  • Plausible directions: vision-language models with metaphor priors (GPT-4V, LLaVA-Next), retrieval over symbol banks, dataset augmentation for zero-shot languages.
  • Code, ablations, and full submission history on the team repo — YZV405E_2526_100.
