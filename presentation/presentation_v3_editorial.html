The Pivot
№ 03
What actually moved the score

The captions beat the vision encoder. By a lot.

Our original SigLIP2 image–text pipeline scored 0.30. Replacing it with BGE-M3 text matching against the English captions jumped to 0.37 — and nothing we tried later could improve on it.

  • SigLIP2's text encoder is weak on non-English and idiomatic input, even after NLLB translation.
  • Image captions, in contrast, are always in English — a clean, single-language channel.
  • BGE-M3 is built for multilingual semantic similarity. Query and captions live in the same space.
  • Adding SigLIP2 image features back on top of BGE captions did not help — text dominates.

Conclusions
№ 06
The visual metaphor gap

Captions describe scenes. Idioms describe concepts. That's the gap.

A figurative image of "bad apple" shows one rotten apple corrupting others. Its caption describes the apples — not the concept of bad influence. No text model bridges that.

What worked i.

  • Classifier-gated query selection — paraphrase for idiomatic, original for literal. +0.07 over the proposed pipeline.
  • BGE-M3 over SigLIP2 for the text channel — multilingual + English caption alignment beats fragmented vision-text.
  • 94.1% sense classification from a single calibrated LR head on top of frozen BGE-M3.
  • Lean compute — 3.5 GB peak VRAM, frozen encoders, full 15-language inference in minutes.

Limits & what's next ii.

  • 0.37 ≪ 0.83 human — six ablation rounds confirmed the ceiling is structural, not engineering.
  • The next jump needs cross-modal reasoning over images, not better text-text similarity.
  • Plausible directions: vision-language models with metaphor priors (GPT-4V, LLaVA-Next), symbol-bank retrieval.
